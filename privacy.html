NeuroTrace V6 research collection

Privacy Policy

Last updated: 24 October 2026 · Consent version: v6.0-2026-10-24

Deployment gate: This policy is a project template, not legal advice or a representation of GDPR compliance. Before cloud collection is enabled, the research controller must publish its legal name, postal address, jurisdiction, a working deletion/contact email, Firebase region and processor details, approved retention workflow, and reviewed security rules. This unconfigured repository build is for internal QA only, not participant recruitment. Cloud collection remains offline until Firebase configuration, complete controller/contact details, reviewed rules, and institutional approvals are supplied.

1. Who is responsible

The research project operator is the data controller for research records. The deployed operator's legal name, postal address, and privacy contact are deployment values, not supplied in this repository. Controller name: not configured; postal address: not configured. Do not submit research data to a deployment that does not identify its controller. Firebase/Google may process data as a service provider under the operator's project and region settings; exact processing locations and contractual terms must be verified by the operator before launch.

Contact for privacy requests: Monitored project email: not configured. The address is intentionally not invented or hard-coded here; collection remains disabled until the project owner configures it.

2. Purpose and legal basis

With your explicit consent, the project collects voluntary handwriting and motor-signal samples and limited self-reported context to develop, evaluate, and audit research models. The software is experimental and does not provide medical care. The operator must document its applicable legal basis and, where required, a GDPR Article 9 condition for processing health-related data before deployment. Consent can be withdrawn for future collection; records already synchronized may require a deletion request.

3. Data collected

  • Drawing trajectories: canvas-relative x/y coordinates, high-resolution browser timing in microseconds, pointer-reported pressure or null when unavailable, tilt, twist, pointer type/id, buttons, pointer dimensions, primary-pointer state, task duration, canvas dimensions, and V5 model output when inference succeeds.
  • Survey data: self-reported neurological status, optional diagnosis duration/stage/medications, age band, biological sex, handedness, tremor conditions, recent caffeine/alcohol, fatigue, self-reported drawing device, and country selected by you.
  • Device/session data: a random UUID stored in this browser, a SHA-256 hash computed from screen resolution, browser user-agent string, and timezone, locale, screen dimensions, pixel ratio, touch capability, pointer observations, a session UUID, and timestamps. We do not request GPS coordinates, names, email addresses, or precise street addresses in the collection form.
  • Consent record: consent version, timestamp, selected consent confirmations, locale, random device UUID, and a separate consent document identifier.
  • Technical delivery: Firebase Authentication's pseudonymous anonymous account ID, Firebase service logs, and (only after consent and project configuration) limited Firebase Analytics app-use events. Drawing trajectories and survey answers are not sent as Analytics event parameters.

SHA-256 hashing does not make a browser fingerprint anonymous: a hash can still distinguish a browser and may be linkable. Device IDs and health information are pseudonymous personal/sensitive data, not anonymous data. Do not enter identifying details in free-text fields.

4. Anonymization and minimization method

The collection avoids asking for names and direct contact details. A random UUID separates research records from direct identity, while a SHA-256 digest represents the specified browser/device attributes. The project does not hold a name-to-UUID lookup in this frontend. This is pseudonymization and data minimization, not irreversible anonymization; records may still be identifiable by linkage, rare survey combinations, network logs, or device fingerprinting. Public release is limited to aggregate statistics with small-cell suppression, but aggregation does not retroactively anonymize the private source records.

5. Consent, age, and voluntary participation

Collection begins only after you confirm that you are at least 18, agree to this policy and the Terms, consent to research donation, and understand that the tool is not a medical diagnosis. You may skip individual drawing tasks or stop before submission. The research form is optional. The collector does not intentionally enroll minors; if you are under 18, do not proceed.

6. Storage, transmission, and security

Drawing events are held in page memory while you draw. Completed submissions are placed in this browser's IndexedDB retry queue before synchronization. IndexedDB is browser-managed local storage; it is not guaranteed encrypted at rest, immune to device access, or permanent. Do not use a shared device. A browser may clear local records or run out of storage.

When configured, consent records are written separately to Firestore collection v6_consents; session records go to v6_sessions. Large trajectory JSON files are stored in private Firebase Storage paths and referenced from the session document; public download URLs are not generated. The dashboard reads only a separate, disclosure-reviewed aggregate document. The repository includes deny-by-default Firestore and Storage rules, but the project operator must deploy and test them, enable appropriate abuse controls (including App Check where suitable), restrict administrative access, and verify backups before accepting data.

Data sent over the network uses HTTPS. No browser-only collection system can guarantee zero data loss or absolute security. Offline queueing, retries, and browser storage are best-effort safeguards, not a backup guarantee.

7. Retention

This repository does not set a retention period for synchronized research data. The responsible controller must publish a reviewed schedule and implement deletion or irreversible aggregation before participant recruitment. The static frontend cannot enforce server-side deletion. Local offline drafts remain in your browser until submitted, cleared using the page controls, or removed by browser storage management.

8. Your rights and deletion

Depending on the law that applies, you may request access, correction, deletion, restriction, portability, or object to processing, and you may lodge a complaint with a supervisory authority. To request deletion of synchronized records, contact the configured privacy email and provide the session or device identifier if you have it; do not send medical details by ordinary email. The project must verify the requester proportionately and respond under applicable law. Use “Withdraw and clear unsent local data” to remove pending drafts and submissions from this browser. Resetting the device ID does not delete queued or already synchronized data.

Consent withdrawal does not invalidate processing already lawfully completed. A withdrawal request should stop future synchronization and initiate deletion review for identifiable records where applicable.

9. International transfers and public dashboard

Firebase project location and any international transfers depend on deployment configuration and must be disclosed by the operator. The dashboard is designed to read only coarse aggregate counts, not individual session documents. Small-cell suppression and re-identification review must be completed before aggregate statistics are published. Country is self-reported and no precise location is collected.

10. Changes and contact

This notice may change as the research protocol changes. Material changes require a new consent version and renewed consent before future collection. The operator must make the current, complete contact information available at deployment. If the responsible controller or contact is absent, do not participate.

11. Medical disclaimer

NeuroTrace is a research prototype, not a medical device. Its model outputs are experimental statistical scores, not diagnoses, treatment recommendations, or evidence of a condition. Do not use them to make health decisions. Consult a qualified health professional for medical concerns.
